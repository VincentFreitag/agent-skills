# Action titles

The title is the slide. Everything below it is evidence. If a reader read only
the titles, they should be able to act.

## The rules

- **State the So What.** "We win 40% of negotiations" is a fact. "Negotiation
  conversion fell from 55% to 40%, tracking the sales-team scale-up" is an
  insight.
- **Present tense, active voice.** "Price drives growth", not "growth was
  driven by price".
- **Quantify.** Numbers make a title credible and stop it drifting into
  platitude. Every number in a title must appear on the slide, identically.
- **Two lines maximum.** Cut filler until it fits. If it will not fit in two
  lines, the slide has two messages — split it.
- **No general truths.** "Managing risk is important for success" says nothing.
- **No absolutes.** "All divisions fail to follow best practice" invites the one
  counter-example that kills your credibility.
- **No antithesis formula.** "It is X, not Y" reads as a flourish and carries half
  a claim, because half the words are spent on the negation. This covers every
  variant: "X, not Y", "not X but Y", "X rather than Y", "not just X but Y",
  "not the other way round". Write the positive claim on its own. If the contrast
  genuinely earns space, give it a second sentence with its own evidence.
  "Lead time follows how new the design is" beats "lead time follows the design,
  not the package". The ban is on output prose. Teaching by counter-example, as
  this file does, is a different job.
- **Results, not process.** Never "we conducted 15 interviews"; always what the
  interviews revealed.
- **Parallel across the deck.** If titles in a section start with a framework
  word, all of them do.

## The kicker convention

Consulting decks routinely prefix the title with a short label and a pipe. It
buys a tracker and a title in one line of space:

```
Historical perf. | A supporting business line with plateauing top-line
proactively seeking diversification chances for long-term sustainable growth

Business plan review | Growth projections at risk from supplier product line
discontinuation; strong need to restructure portfolio for new drivers

Capabilities assessment | While the BU has solid expertise in sales & supply
chain, portfolio strategies & market intelligence must sharpen to unlock growth

Next steps | Two questions guide phase 2: the BU's primary role, and its
implications for portfolio and org strategy
```

The kicker is the section or analysis name (9pt, muted). The clause after the
pipe is the full action title. Use it when a deck has recurring workstreams;
skip it when the storyline is linear.

## The improvement ladder

| Level | Example | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Topic label | "Market dynamics" | Says nothing |
| Generic claim | "Focus on expansion to attractive geographies" | True of any company |
| Wordy insight | "Based on our analysis of margins and growth, we believe Asia Pacific and North America should be prioritised because they have attractive market size" | Right idea, unreadable |
| **Consulting grade** | "Asia Pacific and North America prioritised on margin, growth and 100+ EUR mn market size" | Insight, quantified, one line |

## Worked examples

Drawn from real MBB decks. Note what each title does structurally.

**1. Quantified problem statement, forward-looking**
> If issues are not addressed, the state's health care labor shortage is
> expected to grow ~2x by 2024

Conditional framing creates the burden to act. The multiple ("~2x") is the
memorable number. Slide beneath: a column chart FY17→FY24 with the `~2x` bracket
drawn on it, an implications column, and an economic-cost callout. Title, chart
annotation and callout all reinforce one number.

**2. Sub-titles under a single message**
> MA's healthcare labor shortages expected to double in next 5 years and reach
> 43K … *(left)*
> … leading to significant implications on quality of care and economic output
> *(right)*

The ellipsis pair splits one sentence across two halves of the slide. Use this
when a slide is genuinely two-part — evidence then consequence — without
breaking the one-message rule.

**3. Concession + directive**
> Capabilities assessment | While the BU has solid expertise in sales & supply
> chain, portfolio strategies & market intelligence to be sharpened to unlock
> growth

Acknowledging the strength first makes the criticism survivable in the room.
Structure: *While [what is working], [what must change] to [outcome]*.

**4. Framing the decision, not the answer**
> Next steps | Two key questions to guide growth directions analysis in phase 2:
> defining BU's primary role and its implications on BU portfolio & org strategy

Correct when the work is not finished. Naming the number of questions ("Two")
sets the audience's expectation for the layout below.

**5. Result stated with the mechanism**
> Recent flattened revenue despite group's 10% CAGR growth '22-'24; shrinking
> steel billets & coke offset by fast-growing construction steel

Outcome, contrast, and cause in one sentence. This is the archetype to imitate
for any performance slide.

## Anti-pattern: the IR deck

Investor-relations packs are the standard counter-example. Their titles are
topic labels — "Comparison of financial performance vs peers", "Business
highlights", "Asset quality" — because the audience wants to look values up, not
be told what to think. That is a legitimate genre, and it is *not* what a
consulting recommendation deck does. If a deck's titles could be swapped between
slides without anyone noticing, it has IR titles.

## Title QA

- Read all titles in sequence with nothing else. Does the argument hold? Where
  does it jump?
- Does each title's number appear on its slide, exactly?
- Could this title sit on any other slide in the deck? If yes, it is too generic.
- Does any title state what *was done* instead of what was *found*?
- Does any title lean on "X, not Y" to sound sharp? Delete the negation and see
  whether the remaining half still says something. If it does, ship that.
- Two lines or fewer, all of them?
